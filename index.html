CUT/UP

A cut-up machine after William S. Burroughs & Brion Gysin, Beat Hotel, Paris, 1959. Paste text. Cut. Shake. Read. Repeat.

Source text

Second text (folded in)

Method

The scissors

Shake radius limits how far a slip may drift from where it was cut — a low shake keeps fragments near their neighbours, full shake scatters the whole page. Passes feed the result back through the scissors.

The table

The cut page will land here. Paste a source text and cut it up — “the best writing seems to be done almost by accident.”

each slip's edge marks where it came from: red, the start of the source — blue, the end.

The voice

Rust lets every slip drift in pitch and speed — more rust, more broken machine. The underlayer sings beneath the voice in a foreign prosody: each slip picks its own intonation contour, tones warble and glide toward the next word, questions climb, full stops dive, commas hang and wobble — speech-shaped, but from nowhere on earth.

“When you cut into the present the future leaks out.” — W.S.B.
